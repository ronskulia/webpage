Coffee Mug Caustic

Parallel light rays hit the inside of the mug, reflect, and travel a fixed length. Sweeping the beam across the circle traces a bright nephroid — the caustic you see in your coffee.

Why a fixed length?

Light comes in tilted by α above the table. It hits the vertical mug wall, whose normal is horizontal — so reflection only flips the sideways direction and leaves the downward slope untouched. Every ray keeps descending at the same angle.

To reach the coffee at the bottom, each ray drops the same height h. Same drop + same slope ⇒ the same horizontal run L = h / tan α. The rays leave the wall in different directions but travel the same length — so their landing points fan out into the caustic. That is exactly the fixed-length picture in the 2D view.

Range of lengths: each ray drops points at many lengths at once. Where points pile up, the color gets brighter — that density is the caustic's intensity.
Sweep on: one ray builds the picture up over time. Off: all rays at once.
